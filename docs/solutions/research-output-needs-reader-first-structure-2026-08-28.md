# Research output needs reader-first structure

## Correction

An immediate Standard-mode test returned a thin, repetitive answer dressed up as a report. It recycled two core sources across every section, relied almost entirely on statistics, inserted long citations into sentence flow, and silently chose the research depth. The problem was not too much analysis; it was too little research coverage and useful detail, presented in a format that was still hard to scan.

## Maintainer rule

Research With Receipts must optimize for a non-expert reader as well as an auditor:

1. Ask the user to choose Quick, Standard, or Deep when the request could reasonably support different levels of effort. Explain each option in plain language; choose only when the user delegates or the task is unambiguously narrow.
2. Put citations after the sentence or bullet they support, inside parentheses. Never interrupt a sentence with a long receipt.
3. For a broad Standard landscape, require genuinely distinct evidence lineages and source classes. Two heavily repeated sources are not sufficient merely because they contain many statistics.
4. Match the evidence mix to the question. Combine quantitative scale with qualitative patterns, concrete examples, practices, barriers, and useful guidance when those dimensions matter. Do not let survey percentages become the whole report.
5. Use descriptive sections and substantive bullets. Bullets are a reading aid, not permission to compress away useful detail. Use familiar words, define necessary jargon, keep one main idea per bullet, and fully answer every material research slot.

For broad landscapes, make the breadth rule enforceable: record the research shape and each source's underlying evidence lineage, then reject delivery below five admitted sources, five lineages, and three source types unless a documented public-evidence shortfall is paired with an explicit gap. Mechanical counts cannot determine whether a lineage label is honest or the evidence mix is useful, so those remain mandatory semantic checks.

## Follow-up correction: coverage planning is not an answer-shaped deliverable

`Answer slots` is internal planning language, not a public output contract. Do not expose it as though every research task must be a list of answered questions, and do not add a five-slot validator merely because the internal plan uses that term. A user may want a research report organized by themes, chronology, comparisons, findings, cases, practices, or recommendations.

Use a flexible internal **coverage plan** to prevent omissions. Derive its areas from the requested deliverable and research purpose. The delivered structure must follow the user's requested artifact; it should not reveal or inherit the planner's implementation vocabulary unless that genuinely helps the reader.

## Product installation boundary

Research With Receipts is a sellable public product, not Sally's personal research skill. Its source, description, tests, and distributable package should live in a standalone product repository. Test it through an explicit repository path or isolated install. Do not keep it in Sally's active skill registry, where its intentionally broad public trigger description can intercept her ordinary research work.

The standalone repository and plugin should be named **Agency Research** (`agency-research`). Keep **Research With Receipts** as the first bundled skill and preserve its trigger description. The plugin is the expandable product boundary; the skill is one capability within it. This leaves room for additional agency-oriented research skills without forcing the whole product to inherit one skill's internal planning vocabulary.

The runtime skill should adopt these principles directly rather than depend on another writing or outlining skill being installed.
