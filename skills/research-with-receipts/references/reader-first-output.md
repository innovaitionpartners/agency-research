# Reader-first research output

Write for a non-expert who wants to understand and use the findings, not reconstruct an analyst's notebook.

## Plain-language rules

- Use familiar words and short sentences. Define necessary technical terms the first time they appear.
- Prefer bullets. Keep one main idea in each bullet and lead with the point, not the source or search process.
- Do not summarize away useful detail. A Standard or Deep report must fully cover every material area in its internal plan, usually with several evidence-bearing bullets under each important theme.
- Do not merge distinct coverage areas into one generic “trend” bullet. A broad landscape should feel like a researched briefing, not a synopsis of two surveys.
- Use descriptive `###` subsection names that tell the reader what the group of bullets covers.
- Explain why a finding matters in ordinary language. Do not add consultant-style throat-clearing or force an implication the evidence does not support.
- Put each receipt after the sentence or bullet it supports, in its own parentheses: `([Source title — Publisher, displayed date](direct URL))`.
- When two sources support one bullet, use two separate parenthetical receipts at the end. Never insert a receipt in the middle of a sentence.

## Match the evidence to the question

Choose evidence dimensions from the user's actual job. A landscape may need prevalence, motivations, use cases, qualitative patterns, examples, operating practices, barriers, counterexamples, and implications. A how-to may need tested practices and failure modes more than market percentages. A comparison may need decision criteria and cases where each option breaks down.

Do not force every dimension into every report. Do not let one easy evidence type dominate merely because it is abundant. For broad Standard or Deep research, make missing qualitative evidence, examples, or practice guidance visible rather than filling every section with statistics from the same surveys.

## Quick scan

```markdown
**Depth:** Quick · **Checked:** YYYY-MM-DD

## Answer
- <Direct answer in one to three bullets, with parenthetical receipts.>

## Limits
- <Material uncertainty, scope limit, conflict, or “None material.”>
```

## Standard brief

```markdown
**Depth:** Standard · **Checked:** YYYY-MM-DD

## Bottom line
- <Three to five bullets that answer the question plainly.>

## What the evidence shows
### <Descriptive theme>
- <Two to four supported points, one per bullet, followed by parenthetical receipt or receipts.>

### <A different useful theme or evidence type>
- <Two to four quantitative, qualitative, example, practice, or counterevidence points appropriate to the question.>

### <Continue until every material coverage area is addressed>
- <Add enough evidence-bearing bullets to cover the area; do not stop because the template already contains two themes.>

## Limits and gaps
- <Disagreement, scope limitation, unavailable evidence, or “None material.”>

## What this means
- <Include only when the user asks for a decision, recommendation, tips, or practical implications. Label inference or judgment and cite its factual basis.>
```

## Deep report

```markdown
# <Research title>
**Depth:** Deep · **Checked:** YYYY-MM-DD

## Bottom line
- <Executive synthesis in bullets with parenthetical receipts.>

## Scope and method
- <Approved boundaries, source policy, evidence lanes, and meaningful method limits.>

## What the evidence shows
### <Question family or useful theme>
- <Several supported findings, one per bullet, with epistemic labels where needed and parenthetical receipts. Repeat themes until every material question family is covered.>

## Disagreements and gaps
- <Resolved conflicts, unresolved disagreement, inaccessible evidence, and missing data.>

## Conclusions and recommendations
- <Only what the evidence supports; distinguish inference and requested judgment.>

## Why research stopped
- <Evidence-sufficiency rationale and remaining gaps.>
```
